METHOD STATEMENT FOR WATER NETWORK
Method Statement: Hydrostatic Pressure Testing of Water Pipelines
Purpose and Scope
The purpose of this document is to outline the procedures for conducting a hydrostatic pressure test at 10 Bar to ensure the structural integrity and leak-tightness of the installed water network.
Tools & Equipment
Pressure Pump: High-pressure hydraulic pump (capable of reaching 15-20 Bar).
Pressure Gauges: Two calibrated gauges with a range of 0–16 or 0–20 Bar (for accuracy at the 10 Bar midpoint).
Water Tank: Clean water source for filling the line.
Inlet/Outlet Manifold: Equipped with isolation valves and air release valves.
End Caps/Stoppers: Properly braced temporary bulkheads.
Pre-Testing Requirements
Partial Backfilling: Pipes must be backfilled leaving only the joints and connections exposed for visual inspection.
Cleaning: The pipeline must be flushed to remove any debris or foreign materials.
Isolation: Isolate the test section from existing networks or sensitive equipment using blind flanges or end caps.
Testing Procedure (10 Bar)
Step 1: Filling and Air Venting
Fill the pipeline slowly from the lowest point of the section.
Keep all air release valves at high points open during filling to ensure all air is expelled.
Once water flows steadily without air bubbles, close the air valves.
Step 2: Pressurization
Increase the pressure gradually in stages (e.g., 3 Bar, 6 Bar, then finally 10 Bar).
At each stage, check for any immediate visible leaks or abnormal pipe movement.
Step 3: The Holding Period
Once the pressure reaches the target 10 Bar, disconnect or isolate the pump.
Maintain the test pressure for a duration of 2 hours (or as per project specifications).
Record the pressure readings every 15 or 30 minutes in the test log.
Step 4: Inspection
Conduct a thorough visual inspection along the entire length of the exposed joints.
Any drop in pressure must be evaluated against the "Allowable Leakage" formula. If the drop exceeds the limit (usually >0.2 Bar), the test is considered failed.
Acceptance Criteria
The test is successful if there is no visible leakage at any joint or fitting.
The pressure remains stable at 10 Bar for the duration of the test (within the allowable tolerance of 0.2 Bar or as defined by the consultant).
Sometimes, a decrease in water temperature inside the pipe leads to a slight thermal contraction in volume, which consequently causes a pressure drop.
Depressurization and Safety
Slow Release: After approval, release the pressure slowly. Rapid depressurization can cause surge damage (Water Hammer).
Safety Perimeter: Establish a safety zone around pressurized fitting.
No Adjustments: Strictly forbid tightening bolts or joints while the line is under the 10 Bar pressure.
Documentation
Upon completion, a Pressure Test Report will be signed by the Contractor and the Consultant, including:
Date and time of the test.
Description/Location of the tested section.
Initial and final pressure readings.
Calibration certificates for the gauges used.
