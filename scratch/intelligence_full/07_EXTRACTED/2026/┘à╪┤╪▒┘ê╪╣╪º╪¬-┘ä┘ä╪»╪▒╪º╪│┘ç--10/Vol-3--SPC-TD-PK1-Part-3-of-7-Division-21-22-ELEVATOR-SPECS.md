1.3 	DEFINITIONS
The following terms used on ACE architectural Drawings and in the Specification, are synonymous and may be used interchangeably:
1. 	"Elevator".
Electric Traction Elevators: Elevators in which cars are hoisted by wire ropes using electrically driven traction sheaves and are defined to include driving machines, cars, hoist way doors, guide rails, guide-rail brackets, roping, buffers, counterweights, signals, control systems, electrical wiring within elevator system, and devices for operations, safety, security, required performance at rated speed and capacity, and for complete elevator installation.
Defective Elevator Work: Operation or control system failures, performances below specified ratings, excessive wear, unusual deterioration or aging of materials or finishes, unsafe conditions, the need for excessive maintenance, abnormal noise or vibration, and similar unusual, unexpected, and unsatisfactory conditions.

2.2 	MACHINE AND DRIVE
Geared Machine-Drive:  Worm-geared traction type, with sheave and brake.
Geared Machine-Worm and Shaft: Accurately machined from one piece of high tensile solid forged steel, and provided with approved thrust bearings designed to take thrust of worm in both directions. Worm rim shall be best quality phosphor bronze or other approved material, hobbled accurately after fitting to gear spider. Provide approved means for fixing rim to spider and to prevent possible loosening of unit in operation. Sound isolation pads shall be provided to reduce vibration and noise transmission to building structure.
Geared Machine-Reduction Gear Assembly: Self-lubricating, enclosed in cast iron, oil and dust tight casing, with adequate lubricating oil capacity.
Geared Machine-Brake: Dual independent brakes shall be provided (type of brakes is subject to the ACE engineer’s approval) and shall be noiseless, spring applied, magnetically released, self-cooling and capable of holding car securely under machine speed and load conditions. Brake shall be designed for automatic and instantaneous application immediately after power is interrupted on motor.  Brake springs shall work in compression.
Geared Machine-Manual Brake Release: Provide means for manual release of brake, to allow manual winding of unit in emergencies. Necessary levers and handles shall be handed to the Employer, with instructions for use.  Brake emergency release shall be self-resetting.
Gearless Machine-Drive:  To have traction sheave and brake.
Gearless Machine-Motor: Enclosed, ventilated type, with top speed not exceeding 1500 rpm.
Gearless Machine-Bearings: Sleeve or anti-friction type, properly lubricated, of ample capacity to sustain heavy loads supported by machine, and rigidly mounted in proper alignment. Sleeve bearings shall have automatic lubrication. Sound isolation pads shall be provided to reduce vibration and noise transmission to building structure.
Gearless Machine-Brake: Dual independent brakes shall be provided (type of brakes is subject to the ACE engineer’s approval) and shall be noiseless, spring applied, magnetically released, self-cooling and capable of holding car securely under maximum speed and load conditions. Brake shall be designed for automatic and instantaneous application immediately after power is interrupted on motor.   Brake springs shall work in compression.
Variable Speed A.C. Motor: Induction type, 3-phase, reversible, with electronically controlled speed, using controlled semi-conductor power rectifiers (thyristors) in main circuit and solid-state electronically computed acceleration and deceleration equipment in control circuits. Speed measurement and feedback control shall be through a tach generator mounted on the machine, measuring actual revolutions of drive and comparing with programmed reference level. Deceleration shall be electronically controlled by eddy-current braking, feeding motor winding with thyristor controlled d.c. current. Acceleration and deceleration shall not exceed 1 m/s/s.
Motors shall be rated at a minimum of 180 starts per hour for elevators with speeds exceeding 1 m/sec and a minimum of 150 starts per hour for speeds 1 m/sec and less and for machine room less elevators.
Machine Motor Assembly: Motor, traction sheaves, brake, gearbox and bearings shall be one-unit assembly mounted on common bed plate. Assembly shall be adequately isolated from main building structure by cork or other approved material, to prevent transmission of noise and vibration.
Lifting Tackle: Provide and fix steel joists and hand-operated winch of adequate strength in each machine room for lifting and moving each assembly.
Motor Acceleration and Jerk: Motors shall produce an average acceleration of not less than 0.6 m/sec2 over the total accelerating period except in machine room-less-lifts, service and freight lifts where lower acceleration rates are acceptable. Acceleration shall not exceed 1.5 m/sec2 on acceleration peak. The rate of acceleration shall be adjustable after installation is made and the final adjustment shall not produce any objectionable physiological effects
on the passengers. Jerk rate shall not be less than 1.2 m/sec3 and shall not exceed 2 m/sec3.
Traction and Deflection Sheaves: Traction sheave shall be fine grained cast iron of suitable hardness, accurately grooved for number and size of hoisting ropes, and shall give constant traction and long rope life.  Bearings for sheaves shall be approved type.  Diameter of sheave shall be not less than forty times overall diameter of ropes.  Deflection and secondary sheaves shall be of same material and grooved similarly to traction sheave, and shall be protected by guards extending below machine level, when required.
Chains:  Duplicate heavy duty high tensile multiple leaf chains, with safety factor of at least
10.
Cylinder: Place in a suitable casing of iron or steel. Cylinder casing shall be 100 mm larger in diameter than cylinder. Whole assembly shall be mounted on lowest slab or pit surface and shall not penetrate into ground.
Packing:  Provide special type packing to eliminate leakage under widely variable load conditions.
Provisions shall be made on each hoisting machine such that the lift car can be raised or lowered during emergency by manual operation. Direction of winding corresponding to raising or lowering must be clearly indicated, the hand winding apparatus where detachable shall be mounted in an accessible position inside the machine room (one per room). A notice shall be provided stating the details of the process step by step and indicating that this shall be done only by authorized person.
Automatic Leveling: Maximum tolerance ± 5 mm for passenger lifts and ±10 mm for service lifts. Accuracy shall be maintained regardless of change of load or rope stretch.
Power Regenerative Converter: a means shall be provided for removing regenerated power from the drive system. This power shall be dissipated in resistors or return to the threephase A.C. power supply system. Failure of the system to remove the regenerated power shall cause the drive output to be removed from the hoist motor. The energy generated during the inverter braking for regeneration shall be performed through the following method:
1. For lifts where the product of load times speed is less than 3500 kg.m/s, combined brake unit and discharging resistors or a power converter shall be used according to the manufacturer’s recommendations.
Machine Room-Less Lifts: all machine-room-less lifts shall be powered by gearless machines with permanent magnet synchronous motors mounted in the overhead inside the lift shaft. Neither machines nor controllers are allowed to be placed in the pit. Provide variable voltage variable frequency drives built into the control panel and located inside the shaft next to the machine. A machine access panel shall be built in either the landing door frame or the adjacent wall at the last served floor to facilitate maintenance, and emergency applications.
2.3 	CAR
Construction: Structural steel frame members suitably braced and fastened together, and clad internally and externally with sheet metal at least 1.5 mm thick, which shall not drum during normal operation.  Materials shall be fire resistant.
Reinforce roof to sustain 150-kg load on any 0.2 m2, and 50 kg at any point. Provide trap door at least 500 x 600 mm, opening outward and capable of being opened from both sides. Opening of trap shall activate electrical interlock preventing operation of elevator. Opening of trap shall activate electrical interlock preventing operation of elevator.
Platform: Fill frame with timber sub-floor and provide specified top floor finish.  Protect underside of platform with sheet steel covering, at least 1.5 mm thick. Sill shall be anti-slip, with provision for car doors.
Load Weighing Device:  Build into floor.
Isolating Cushion: Rubber pads supported on auxiliary steel frame fastened to car frame shall form isolating cushion between car and car frame.
Balustrade and Kick-Plates shall be fitted to car roof in all lifts as per code requirements.
Bumper Rails and Kick Plates: shall be fitted inside the car of the freight elevators. They are provided for service elevators if requested in elevator data sheets.
Bumper Rails and kick-plates fitted to car shall not be less than 75 mm and 150 mm wide respectively.
Ventilation and Lighting: each car shall have necessary lighting and adequately rated, silent running, properly padded and rigidly fixed exhaust fan of approved type and manufacturer. Fan and lights shall be switched on automatically when car is called or when landing doors are opened, remain switched on whilst car is in operation, and switched off by time delay 30 - 60 seconds after car has stopped. Lighting level shall be minimum 100 lux. Fan shall be provided to bring air into car through the ventilation slots near the wall base and to exhaust through the ceiling. Fan shall be 2-speed to provide at least 1 air change a minute at low speed and 1.5 air change a minute at high speed. A screen grill over the fan shall be provided for mechanical protection of fan. Lighting type and arrangement is subject to ACE engineer’s approval. Battery shall be provided to operate fan and two of the lighting fixtures for at least one hour.
Natural ventilation shall be provided for each car irrespective of provision of forced ventilation.
Padded Covers: Provide one set with each type of service elevator, to protect car finishes from damage at times elevator is used to carry freight.  Provide hooks for hanging padded covers.
Noise Level Inside the Car shall not exceed the following values:
50 dBA at full speed in both directions with fan off,
55 dBA at full speed in both directions with fan on at high speed.
Noise Level outside Car shall not exceed 55 dBA when measured at one meter in front of car entrance during a full door open and close cycle and a reversal cycle, in addition to a lift traveling at full speed inside the shaft.
Car Horizontal Acceleration: Lateral quaking, peak to peak, shall not exceed 15 milli-g.
Car Inspection Station shall be provided on top of the car to include the following:
Two-position (auto/inspection) switch.
Run button, to be pressed in conjunction with up and down direction buttons.
This to prevent accidental running under inspection.
Stop switch.
Duplex electrical socket standard type.
Work light to be either plugged into the electrical socket or directly wired into the top of car station. If directly wired "off/on" switch is required on the top of car station.
When the station is switched to inspection, the lift speed shall not exceed 0.63 m/s and all operating devices in the car shall be inoperative.
P. Notices and Signage: A stainless steel grade 304 load plate shall be provided inside the car indicating the following;
Rated load.
Name of company, telephone number and emergency instruction.
Fire instructions “In case of fire do not use lift”.
Q. Car Finish: Unless otherwise specified in elevator data sheets, car finishes shall be as follows:
Walls: etched stainless-steel grade 304(subject to ACE engineer’s approval)
Doors: hairline stainless steel grade 304 (subject to ACE engineer’s approval)
Ceiling: stainless steel grade 304 (subject to ACE engineer’s approval)
Handrail: stainless steel grade 304 at rear wall (subject to ACE engineer’s approval)
Floor: heavy duty granite (subject to ACE engineer’s approval)
Lighting: LED light for passenger lifts and fluorescent for service & bed lifts (subject to ACE engineer’s approval)
R. Loading Class: Freight elevators shall have loading class C3 unless loading class C1or C2 are specified in elevator data sheets. For class C1 loading, concentrated wheel loading on car and landing sills of up to 80% of rated load is never to be exceeded.
2.4 	AUXILIARIES
Counterweight: Structural steel frame fitted with cast iron sub-weights secured with tie rods or steel sub-weights with locks at the top. Weight shall be equal to total car weight plus 40 to 50% of contract specified capacity. Counterweight shall be screened to a height of 2 m from bottom of pit and fitted with renewable guide shoes at top and bottom. Blocking shall be provided between counter weight and buffer strike plate for at least 250 mm per 60 rope length.
Guide Shoes: shall be rolling type for speeds exceeding 1.6 m/sec and sliding type for speeds 1.6 m/sec and below. Guide shoes shall be adjustable, renewable, and fastened to top and bottom of car and counterweight frames.
Roller Guide Shoes: roller guide shoes shall be adjustable mounting base type, rigidly bolted to the top and bottom of each side of car and counterweight frame. Roller guides shall consist of set of sound reducing rubber-covered wheels with precision ball bearings held in contact with the three finished rail surfaces by adjustable stabilizing springs. Roller guides shall be equipped with adjustable stops to control past wise float. The car and counterweight roller guides shall not rotate at more than 400 rpm and 800 rpm respectively to maximize roller guide life and car ride comfort. Statically balance the car and counterweight to maintain uniform pressure on the roller guides.
Guide Rails: highest quality steel T-sections with tongue and grove joints for the car and counterweight. Guide rails sections shall be connected with machine fish plates. Unless otherwise indicated, use car and counterweights guide rails with a suitable rail sizes to BS EN 81-1 and profiles as per ISO standards rail sections. When rail length exceeds 30 m means shall be provided to absorb rail expansion and structural settlement. Guide rail fixings shall be heavy steel brackets using metal C-inserts in concrete walls. Guide rail and fixings shall withstand sudden application of safety gear under maximum load.
Inserts: coordinate with builder's works to furnish required concrete inserts and similar anchorage devices for the installation of guide rails, machinery, and other components of lift work where installation of devices is indicated as work. Provide necessary shop drawings for the required works.
Suspension Ropes: special acid quality steel or high grade traction steel, single factory production patch with safety factor above 14 or as specified by BS EN 81-1 whichever is greater. Ropes shall be securely attached to car and counterweight and shall have independent means of adjustment. Provide test certificates of compliance with appropriate Code and/or Standard prior to shipment. For travels above 60 m, provide steel cord governor ropes to limit movement of the governor tension wheel due to changing humidity values. The minimum number of ropes is three. An automatic tensioning device shall be provided to equalize the tension of suspension ropes.
Roping Arrangement: roping arrangements used are subject to verification by the manufacturer and approval of the ACE engineer.
Compensation Ropes: provide compensation for the weight of the hoist way ropes and unbalanced portion of the traveling cables whenever the travel exceeds 30 m so that the system balance does not vary by more than 50 kg over the entire range of travel.
Compensation Ropes: compensation shall consist of steel wire ropes for speeds 2.5 m/sec & above and chain with a fire retardant round resilient rubber cover for rated speeds less than 2.5 m/sec.
Compensation Ropes: compensation ropes shall be attached to the underside of the car and to the counterweight passing under a weighed compensation sheave in the pit. Provide lockdown as described in BS EN 81-1 to the compensation sheave assembly, whenever rope compensation is applied. Provisions shall be made for equalizing tension in the compensating ropes. The guide sheave shall be provided with bearings of anti-friction bearing metal, ball or roller type. The guide sheave shall operate in guides, and the guide structure shall be securely fastened in place. Provide metal guards to prevent ropes jumping off sheave and to prevent foreign objects from falling between ropes and sheave grooves and to protect workmen. Provide upper and lower travel limit switched on the compensation guide sheave. When actuated, power to the drive and brake shall be removed.
Buffers: buffers shall be of approved design, oil type for speeds exceeding 1 m/sec and spring or polyurethane type for speeds of 1 m/sec and below unless otherwise indicated. Buffers shall be installed under each car and counterweight. Use reduced stroke buffers with associated terminal slowdown devices where runbys are restrictive. Buffers shall be tested by a qualified testing laboratory and approved as complying with the governing code.
Oil Buffers: provide oil buffers with necessary blocking and horizontal steel braces. Oil buffers shall bring the car and counterweight to rest from governor tripping speed at an average rate of retardation not exceeding gravity. Oil buffers shall be of the spring return type and shall have means of the checking oil supply level and shall provide a limit switch as a backup for the oil level. Oil buffers shall have a marking plate indicating the manufacturer's name, the buffers identification number and the stroke.
Spring Type or Polyurethane Buffers shall be in full compliance with the code requirements. Buffers shall be designed and constructed to absorb within the limits of their stroke, the whole kinetic energy of the car carrying its rated load when the speed of impact is the maximum governor tripping speed.
Governor Actuated Mechanical Safety Gear: mechanical safety gear shall be mounted on and securely fastened to car frame and actuated by a governor mounted in machine room. Governor shall be directly driven by steel cable or chain. Safety gear and governor shall be in compliance with BS EN 81:1.
Safety Gear: safety gear shall hold car securely under maximum load and governor tripping speed conditions. Jaws shall engage guide rails through self-aligning shoes with surfaces carefully machined parallel to guide rail face. Pressure of jaws on the two guides shall be equal. Safety gear shall bring car to stop from governor tripping speed. Make provisions to release the car safety. In no event the safety shall be released by downward motion of the car. Raising the car to reset the safety gear shall be allowed. An electrical safety plank switch shall be provided to interrupt the power to the hoist machine when the safety gear is set.
Safety Gear shall be type tested in accordance with BS EN 81-1 by an approved independent test body.  Certified type test report shall be submitted for approval.  Install a car safety marking plate of corrosion resistant metal and, in addition to the data required by the governing code, indicate the manufacturer's name and manufacturer's catalogue designation number for the safety gear.
Governor shall be accurately adjusted and sealed to trip safety gear according to BS EN 81-1 requirements. Operation of governor on over speed is also a switch disconnecting power from motor and brake control circuits, which shall be open before safety gear is applied. Restoration of power shall not be possible until safety gear has been manually reset. Governor shall be sealed and tagged in accordance with BS EN 81-1.
Counterweight shall have safety gear for all elevators that have an occupied space below the pit.
Governor Ropes: the over speed governor shall be driven by a wire rope designed for that purpose with minimum diameter of 6 mm and shall be easily detachable from the safety gear. The ratio between the pitch diameter of the pulleys for the over speed governor ropes and the nominal rope diameter shall be at least 30. The over speed governor rope shall be tensioned by a tensioning guided pulley. Design the governor rope-tripping device so that no appreciable damage to or deformation of the governor rope shall result from the stopping action of the device in operating the car safety.
Safety Gear Type shall be progressive (gradual) type if the lift rated speed is 1 m/sec or above and progressive (gradual) or instantaneous type if the lift rated speed is below 1 m/sec unless otherwise indicated.
Lift Pit Stop Switch: A switch shall be provided at each lift pit to stop the lift and prevent it from any further movement until placed back to the “Run” position.
Pit Access: An access door or cat ladder shall be provided for the access of the elevator pit according to code requirements.
2.5 	DOORS
Opening Sizes:  Landing and car door openings shall be of the same size.
Automatic Sliding Car and Landing Doors: power operated type, open silently, and shall have the following features:
Hangers and tracks of two-point suspension ball or roller bearing type; doors guided at bottom by gibes in grooved sill; grooves and tracks cleaned automatically by operation of doors.
All landing doors shall be insulated, 2-hours fire rated as per EN 81-58 and EN 1363 standards. Criteria of door performance shall satisfy both integrity (E) and thermal insulation (I) tests. For the low rise buildings, 6 floors and less; non-insulated, 2-hours fire rated doors satisfying integrity test (E) can be used and as per coordination with life safety project.
Infrared light-beam (minimum of 35 beams) shall open doors when light-beam is interrupted and to automatically optimize time of opening and reclosing through microprocessor-based controller. Alternative protection devices may be used for service elevators, details shall be provided for ACE engineer’s review and approval.
Include extended door hold open button in the main car operating panel for all service and freight elevators to extend the door open time (10-20 sec, time to be adjustable through proper programing) to facilitate cart transfer. Registration or re-registration of a car call shall cancel the hold open time.
Doors shall open automatically and simultaneously when car stops at required floor, shall remain open for a reasonable, adjustable period, and automatically and simultaneously close, as set in lift control sequence.
'Door open' push button on car control panel shall reverse door closing operation, but shall not open an interlocked door.
Door contact to prevent operation of the car unless the car door is in the closed position. The door contacts are not be readily accessible from the inside of the car.
Nudging facility so that if the doors are held open for a predetermined time (15 to 20 seconds) by interrupting the light beams or by holding the door, or by pressing the door open button, a buzzer shall sound and the doors shall start to close at reduced rate of speed. Doors shall continue to close unless the door force limits is not exceeded, which shall cause doors to reopen or stop.  Process shall continue until obstruction is removed from entrance.
Adjustable door dwelling time, the time interval that elevator doors stand open after a stop shall be independently adjustable for car call stops and hall call stops. A separate door time shall be adjustable for lobby door hall calls.
Advance door opening, the car and hall doors shall begin to open while the car is preparing to stop at a landing to answer a call. The distance from a landing at which the doors begin to open shall comply with applicable codes.
C. Electric Door Operator driven by a heavy duty electric motor, minimum IP 23 for firefighting lifts and IP 21 for other lifts, shall open and close car and landing doors simultaneously. Door operation shall be automatically controlled by lift control system, with facility to manually reverse door closing operation from car control panel. Doors shall be mechanically secured and electrically interlocked when closed.  Electric door operator shall have the following features:
Variable voltage variable frequency (VVVF) electric motor driving unit, mounted on car top (vibration isolated from car body) to operate the car and hoist way doors simultaneously, with necessary gears, levers, door arms, belts and ropes to achieve smooth operation of doors in both directions and an opening speed higher than closing speed.
Digital closed-loop system to monitor and control the door speed.
Torque limiting clutch to prevent harm to persons caught by closing door, but to overcome small obstructions in bottom guide track.
Manual opening of doors shall be possible in the event of power failure, if car is at landing, landing door shall open with car door; mechanism shall reset automatically.
With installations, in which doors commence opening while car is leveling to landing, mechanical and electrical devices shall ensure no dangerous situation can occur during this period.
Emergency Doors: for express runs exceeding 11m, provide emergency landing door every 11m. Emergency landing doors shall be automatic, sliding, central opening, of same type, size and finish of the landing doors of the same lift group, 2 hours fire rated and shall be provided by the lift supplier.
Door Frames and Sills: door support units and sill support fitted at landing entrance shall be self- contained and provided by lift contractor, comprising sill, two angle uprights and head. Non-slip sill shall be fitted at each landing and car entrance. Sills shall be made of extruded hard aluminum with rubbish disposal halls.
Sills shall be made of solid stainless-steel grade 304 for freight elevators with loading classC1.
2.6 	VARIABLE VOLTAGE VARIABLE FREQUENCY (VVVF) DRIVE CONTROLLER
Motor Drive: unless otherwise indicated or specified, use 3-phase, solid state, variable voltage variable-frequency (VVVF) power drive with regulated closed loop speed monitoring control system. Drive shall utilize IGBT power semi-conductors having means for heat dissipation and overheat protection (include thermal overload).
Continuous Operation: drive shall be designed for continuous operation at rated speed and rated load, and for 10 minutes at rated speed and 110 percent of rated load, without overheating. Ensure that, in such operating conditions, normal power sources are not adversely affected and emergency generator circuits (and loads connected to the emergency power bus) are not adversely affected during standby power operation. Protect each power feed line against surge currents. Prevent runaway in the event of closed loop feedback circuit failure.
Electronic Negative Feedback System: shall be used to limit current through IGBT’s and motor, incorporating:
Pattern-generator giving a velocity input signal modified by car position, with constant peak acceleration and constant change of acceleration.
Encoder to provide a velocity feedback signal.
Digital transducer to provide a position feedback signal.
Current transformer to provide a current feedback signal.
Power line noise shall comply with IEC 61000-3-4. Necessary filters shall be provided to limit the feedback current harmonics to standard limits.
Overall Drive System Response: arrange system response so that the elapsed time between fault detection and removal of electric power does not exceed 0.1 second. Overall drive efficiency shall be greater than 95 percent. Power factor shall be 0.95 minimum.
Power supply equipment shall be designed to operate at ± 10 percent supply voltage and ± 3 percent supply frequency, without damage to any equipment and to operate at ± 5 percent supply voltage and ± 3 percent supply frequency without affecting operational performance quality. Protective devices shall be provided for both over-voltage and under-voltage conditions. Protect against loss of phase, phase reversal or overload. Incoming power supply shall be provided with contactor inside the control panel.
Controller: the controller shall be microprocessor-based and provided with the following operating features and performance functions:
Digital control, with adjustments that do not vary, drift or age with line voltage changes.
Linearity between required torque and torque output.
Precise closed-loop speed, regulated to less than ± 1% by frequency compensation.
S-curve ‘soft’ start/stop, with a fast speed response system.
Diagnostic record of the history of the lift operating conditions, from the instant of the last fault occurrence back 30 days’ minimum, and to include the following as a minimum:
Emergency Faults.
Fire service activation.
Inspection mode.
Independent service.
Time out of service.
Car out of service.
Safety edge failure.
Emergency power.
Application-specific memory to support logic function programming, to tailor the drive.
Quiet motor operation, based on high switching frequencies and low harmonic content.
Full function operator interface with alphanumeric display. Include: a menu structure with scrolling to reveal system options and display parameters; and interactive interface to permit display and ready changing of parameter values; constant pressure jog for pre-set speed control; motor rotation direction selector; run/stop commands.
Protective features including phase-to-phase and phase-to-neutral short-circuit protection; ground fault protection; electronic motor overload protection; overtorque and under-torque detection; over current, over-voltage ad overtemperature detection; input/output phase loss.
Noise filter between hoist way wiring and controller to eliminate interference.
H. Protection: system shall be protected against the following:
An error level exceeds the signal by more than 5 percent.
Actual acceleration exceeds a pre-set pattern more than 15 percent.
Excessive transistor switching time or overheating of solid-state components.
Car positions as derived from a digital transducer and an integrated velocity feedback signals differ by more than 12 mm.
No voltage or sustained under voltage.
Over current in any component.
Phase reversal of power supply.
Wiring: install copper wiring runs shall be neatly installed. Wire shall be terminated at studs or terminal strips, with connections to ensure substantial electrical and mechanical integrity. All major components shall be exactly identified as indicated on wiring diagrams by engravings or tags mounted immediately adjacent to the component.
Enclosure shall be a sheet metal cabinet totally enclosed, IP 21 with removable hinged doors (key- locked) and with filtered ventilation sufficient to maintain interior temperature at a reasonable level.
Auxiliary Emergency Supply: control system shall operate from rectified AC supply, at a suitably selected and approved DC voltage. Negative terminal shall be connected to earth. Battery shall be sealed nickel cadmium or lithium type, capable of operating intercom, car fan, half of the car lights and alarm circuits for 1 hour, in the event of power failure.

2.9 	CONTROL SYSTEMS
Interlock: No car shall be able to move unless car and corresponding landing doors are fully closed and interlocked. It shall not be possible to open landing door, except that at which car is stopped, and any other door without the use of special tamperproof key.
Anti-Nuisance Feature: in the event of car loading or operation is not commensurate with the number of car calls registered, all calls shall be cancelled.
Operation from Emergency Power Batteries: If the normal power supply fails, lifts shall automatically switch to battery operation. Each lift shall move in the "light" direction, system shall rely on releasing the motor break, thus by allowing the elevator to move in the most favorable direction according to the counterweight location and the elevator location (Up or Down) and shall stop at the nearest available floor, open its doors for passengers to exit and then remain on this floor with its doors opened, all safety features shall remain operational during system operation. When normal power is restored or emergency power from generator supply operates before the lift has reached the next available floor, the lifts operating on emergency battery power shall stop at the next available floor and be automatically removed from service. Emergency battery power is then to be disconnected and normal power or emergency power from the generator shall be applied to the lifts.  In case of emergency supply is connected; only one lift of each group in addition to all fire fighter lifts shall be operated from the emergency power supply and shall respond to car and landing calls. After restoration of normal power supply all lifts shall resume normal service automatically.  Batteries shall be sealed lead acid type.
Excess Load: Load-weighing device installed in car shall prevent car from starting in either direction if maximum loading is exceeded (adjustable from 100% - 110% of full load capacity and set at 100%). Buzzer shall sound and an 'overweight' light shall be illuminated until car is below maximum load. Excess load shall cause car to by-pass landing calls if car is loaded above normal but within an acceptable load limit.
Overload Bypass: excess load shall cause car to by-pass landing calls if car is loaded above normal but within an acceptable load limit.  Load capacity shall be set at 70% (adjustable from 60 - 80% of full load capacity).
Distributed Parking: When cars are not required for response to calls, they are parked with doors closed and distributed in predetermined zones throughout the building. One zone shall include the main floor and adjacent floors; remaining floors shall be divided into approximately equal zones.
Independent Service: Keyswitch in car-control station removes car from group operation and allows it to respond only to car calls. Key cannot be removed from keyswitch when car is in independent service. When in independent service, doors close only in response to door close button.
Fire Fighter Lifts: only fire fighter lifts can resume operation under fire emergency conditions from car push buttons by activation of the car fire emergency key switch. The car fire emergency switch shall be effective only when the hall fireman key switch at the main landing floor is in the "on" position or the fire alarm detection device (if provided) is activated and the car is at the main landing floor. The car key shall be removable only in the "off" position. When the car key switch is in the "on" position, it shall bring the lift into the emergency mode. Emergency operation from inside the car shall be cancelled when the hall fireman key switch is switched to the "off" position with the car at the main landing floor
Fire Fighter Lifts: operation under fire conditions shall be as follows after activation of the car fire emergency key switch:
The lift shall be operable only by a person in the car.
The lift shall not respond to landing floor calls.
The opening of power operated doors shall be controlled only by continuous pressure of the "open" button and if the button is released prior to the doors reaching the fully open position, the doors shall automatically reclose. Open doors shall be closed by either the registration of a car call button or by pressure on door "close" button.

2.10 	LIFT OPERATION MODES
General: Provide manufacturer's standard microprocessor operation systems as required to provide type of operation indicated.
Collective-Selective Group Control: Each elevator group shall operate under one control system, handling all landing calls collectively and selectively. It shall be possible for each elevator to be switched to attendant operation, removing it from the group. Elevator control system shall be so arranged that appropriate car responds to landing calls by automatic selection, and systematic spacing of cars is maintained to minimize delays. Direction in which car will travel shall be determined by calls stored in system. 'Up' and 'down' directional call pushes shall be provided at each landing served by Mall, together with 'call registered' illuminated signal for each push.  'Up' or 'down' push only shall be provided at terminal landings. In each car, a push shall be provided for each floor served, marked with appropriate floor designation. When elevators are switched to passenger operation, calls by momentary pressure on 'floor' pushes in car or pushes at landings shall be registered in control system and automatically and selectively cause closing of respective car door and start car in direction of first registered call. Elevator shall collectively answer registered car and landing calls in sequence in one direction and then in the other. Having answered all calls stored in system, one car shall return automatically to park at main floor, with doors open, and other car(s) shall remain at floor last reached. When any elevator of a group is switched to attendant operation, car shall respond to car calls and landing calls stored in system, but car shall start when attendant presses 'start' button. When attendant presses 'pass' button, landing calls are by-passed but remain registered. When attendant presses 'reverse' button, car shall stop and travel in opposite direction. Unanswered calls shall remain registered. 'Pass' and 'reverse' buttons shall be inoperative in passenger operation mode.
Supervisory System For Group Control: Elevators shall operate as one group by a microprocessor controlled supervisory system, automatically regulating movement of cars according to various traffic requirements, classified in five conditions: System automatically adjusts to demand changes for different traffic conditions including heavy incoming, heavy two-way, heavy outgoing, and light off-hours as variations of normal two-way traffic.
'Off-peak and balanced traffic':  Generally, two way and inter-floor traffic.
'Up-peak': when traffic is predominantly incoming.
'Down-peak': when traffic is predominantly outgoing.
'Light traffic': when traffic is intermittent such as outside normal working hours.
'Night service'.
Selection of traffic condition and regulation of movement of cars shall be made automatically according to number and frequency of calls registered in system. In no case are two elevators to answer same directional landing call.
D. Supervisory System for Group Control:
During 'off-peak', elevators shall operate as group collective-selective.
During 'up-peak', all elevators, except one, shall answer only car calls, and travel directly back to main floor immediately after all calls have been answered; one elevator shall also answer 'up' and 'down' landing calls.
During 'down-peak', all elevators, except one, shall answer car calls and landing 'down' calls, and travel directly up to highest registered landing call immediately they become free at main landing; one elevator shall also answer 'up' landing calls; 'down-peak' operation shall incorporate feature which automatically divides operation into two stages:
Stage 1:   Dispatching of cars shall be modified to suit prevailing outof-balance demand.
Stage 2:  If out-of-balance trend continues or becomes more predominant a 'zoned operation' shall be brought in.
During 'zoned operation', car or cars in lower zone are, in their upward journey, to operate with high call reversal; 'zoned operation' shall continue until Stage 2 peak condition ceases, when low cars shall revert to Stage 1 operation.
During 'light traffic', motor generator sets shall shut down after outstanding calls have been answered, and for a predetermined time when call is registered; motor generator of one car shall restart when call is registered, and others shall restart when calls become more frequent.
Arrangements shall be incorporated so that any one car may be left in operation for 'night service'; dispatching system is then to be inoperative and car shall start in response to first car or landing call.
E. Supervisory System for Group Control: At least one car shall be brought back to main floor and remain there with doors open, car lights on and 'this elevator' sign illuminated.

Set sills flush with finished floor surface at landings. Coordinate with other trades to facilitate and ensure proper grouting of sills.
